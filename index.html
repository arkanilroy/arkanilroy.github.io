About

I'm a postdoctoral researcher in the Klem Research Group at Louisiana State University. My background is in computational chemistry, where I use quantum mechanics, molecular dynamics, and machine learning to study how enzymes work at the atomic level — and where that mechanism breaks down in disease.

My Ph.D. work under Dr. G. Andrés Cisneros focused on DNA polymerases: modeling the structural and catalytic effects of disease-associated mutations, comparing metal-ion dependence, and developing new computational methods for multipole-based force fields. That work has led to publications in Science Advances, JCTC, JCIM, and Protein Science.

Research interests

DNA polymerase mechanisms

Structural and catalytic effects of disease-associated mutations, studied via hybrid QM/MM and MD.

Multiscale simulation

QM/MM, molecular dynamics, and transition-path methods to capture reaction mechanisms and conformational change.

Computational method development

Improving force-field electrostatics through the Gaussian Electrostatic Model (GEM).

Structural bioinformatics

Homology and ML-based structure prediction (RosettaFold, AlphaFold) applied to enzyme systems.
